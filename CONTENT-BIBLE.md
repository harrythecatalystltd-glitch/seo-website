## 1. The Catalyst Method

The named framework. Owned by Harry. Use this name for the process, the programmes, and the
structure behind any lead magnet.

### The whole thing in one line

> **Catch it. Let it go. Ask what's true. Do your twenty minutes. Back yourself, don't beat
> yourself.**

Every step is an instruction, not a label. That is deliberate, and it comes straight from Harry's
own argument in the mental state checklist post: *"A checklist is neither. It is an instruction.
Be curious is not a claim I have to believe about myself. It is something I can simply do."* You
cannot fail an instruction. You can only follow it or not.

### Read this before the list

The five steps are not five versions of the same thing. They work at three different speeds, and
saying which is which is most of what makes the method easy to understand.

- **Steps 1 to 3 are what you do when a feeling turns up.** About a minute, in the moment.
- **Step 4 is what you do every day**, whether anything happened or not.
- **Step 5 is what keeps step 4 alive** over months and years instead of weeks.

### The five steps

**1. Catch it**
Notice the feeling before you start the story about it.
Something tightens. Your mood drops. You get irritated. That is the moment, before you work out
whose fault it is.
*You have done it when:* you can name the feeling. Fear. Frustration. Embarrassment.
*Gets easier with:* counting your breath to ten, or a twenty minute walk with no phone.

**2. Let it go**
Find where it sits in your body, and stay there until it runs out.
Chest, stomach, throat, jaw. Do not argue with it, do not push it down, do not go and tell
someone about it. Just let it be there. The energy behind a feeling is limited, and it burns out
if you stop fighting it.
*You have done it when:* it has faded, and you did nothing clever to make it fade.
*This is the engine. Nothing after it works without it.*

**3. Ask what's true**
Separate what actually happened from what your head added on top.
Something happened. Then you added a meaning to it. Usually the meaning is bigger and more
personal than the event was. Ask yourself which of the two you are actually reacting to.
*You have done it when:* you can say the plain facts out loud with the meaning stripped off.

**4. Do your twenty minutes**
Every day, do one thing that actually grows you.
Meditation, a walk with no phone, reading, learning something, moving your body. It has to mean
something to you. Forced self-improvement rarely survives past day three.
This step is the experiencing and the doing. Not thinking about it, not planning it, not reading
another book about it. Doing it.
On the days it feels too big, do five minutes. Never nothing.
*You have done it when:* you did it. Not when you understood why it matters.

**5. Back yourself, don't beat yourself**
Hold yourself to what you said you would do, out of respect rather than force.
Discipline means controlling and starving yourself into behaving. It works for a few weeks, then
it cracks, and you decide the crack proves something about you. It does not. You just ran out of
a thing that always runs out.
Keep your word to yourself because you respect yourself enough to keep it, not because you will
punish yourself if you do not.
Then stay consistent, and stay persistent, past the point where it stops being interesting.
And check what is actually driving it. Chasing a goal because you desperately want it is force,
and force runs out. Doing it because your intention is good and your decisions are good is what
holds.
*You have done it when:* you kept going through a week that gave you nothing back.

### What it looks like in real life

Use this worked example, or one like it, whenever the method is being explained to someone new.
It is the fastest way to make it click.

**In the moment.** You are about to send the message. Your chest tightens, and a thought turns up:
they will think I am an idiot.

> **Catch it.** That is a feeling, not a fact. It is fear.
> **Let it go.** It is in your chest. Sit with it. Do not argue with it. Wait. It fades.
> **Ask what's true.** What actually happened is that you wrote a message. The rest is a story
> you added on top of it.
>
> Then you send it.

**That evening, and every day after.**

> **Do your twenty minutes.** A walk with no phone. This is the bit that makes you catch it
> faster next time, and it happens whether or not anything went wrong that day.
> **Back yourself, don't beat yourself.** On the day you skip it, do five minutes instead of
> deciding you are someone who never finishes anything.

### Why the order matters

This is what makes it a method rather than a list, and it is worth repeating often.

**Most advice starts at step 4.** Just take action, and the confidence catches up. That works
right up until the feeling is loud enough to stop you leaving the house.

- Try to talk yourself into a better thought before you have let the feeling go, and you are just
  **hiding the feeling under a nicer sentence**.
- Act before you have dealt with the feeling, and you are pushing against it the whole way.
- Do the first three and skip step 4, and you have had a nice insight that changes nothing.
- Do step 4 on discipline instead of step 5, and it cracks in about six weeks, and you decide the
  crack proves something about you.

Clear the feeling. Check the story. Then do the daily thing, and hold yourself to it out of
respect rather than force. That is the whole claim, and it is the one thing here that is
genuinely Harry's.

## 2. Core beliefs

These are non-negotiable. Every piece of content should be traceable to at least one of them,
and must contradict none of them.

**On feelings and thoughts**

1. Your negative thoughts and feelings are not you. They arrived to help you survive, not to make
   you happy. They are not a verdict on who you are.
2. There are only four things you can do with a difficult feeling: suppress it, vent it, escape
   it, or let it go. The first three manage it. Only the fourth removes it.
3. The energy behind any feeling is limited. It runs out if you stop fighting it.
4. Underneath most stuck feelings sits one of three wants: approval, control, or security.

**On truth and perspective**

5. Your current perspective is not reality. It is a lens made of your mood, your history and your
   fears.
6. Essence is what happened. Appearance is the story added on top. Most suffering is a reaction to
   appearance treated as essence.
7. Fear, worry and feeling not good enough are false realities. They are leftover perspectives,
   not evidence.
8. People cannot help the way they are. They are shaped by upbringing and unexamined fear. That
   explains behaviour, it does not excuse harm. The useful response is honesty, not judgement.
9. The test for whether a thought is true: does holding it make you feel stronger and more open,
   or smaller and tighter.

**On confidence and self-belief**

10. **You are enough as you are.** Right now, not once you have fixed something. This is the
    single most important sentence on the site.
11. You do not need to find anything. Belief is not collected, it is stopped being blocked.
12. Self-belief is the belief that you will do what you say you will do. Nothing grander.
13. Self-belief is about you. Confidence is about the task. Build self-belief first, confidence
    follows from it.
14. Self-belief runs on evidence, which is why affirmations on their own feel hollow.
15. Your ego is built to keep you the same, because sameness is safe. That is why the doubting
    thoughts keep arriving.
16. Self-doubt gets loudest right before you grow. Volume tells you proximity, not accuracy.

**On change and daily practice**

17. Discipline is a negative mindset. It means controlling, starving and forcing yourself, and it
    runs on the assumption that without force you would be lazy. That fuel burns out.
18. What lasts is self-respect and accountability held out of love rather than punishment. Same
    actions, different engine.
19. Doing comes first. Belief follows after.
20. Twenty minutes a day, done because it means something to you, beats any overhaul.
21. On the days it feels too big, shrink it. Never skip it. A five-minute walk still counts.
22. It is like going to the gym. The value is in the routine, not in any single session.
23. Stuck is not the same as lost. Stuck is what happens when you stop letting go daily and the
    unreleased quietly piles up until it runs the show.
24. **Some stuck periods are capacity, not failure.** When life is genuinely full, young kids, a
    hard job, an ill parent, the practice is the first thing to go, and stuck follows from the
    practice stopping. That is a sequence, not a character flaw, and the honest answer is often
    that the season has to change before the practice can come back.

    *This sits underneath belief 23, not against it.* Stopping the daily work is still what
    causes stuck. Capacity is what causes the stopping. Say both, in that order, and never use
    capacity as a reason to tell someone nothing can be done. The smaller version is always
    available. See belief 21.

**On why this exists**

25. The more you do this work, the more you want to give instead of take.
26. Helping someone see the truth about themselves is one of the most useful things you can offer
    anyone.
27. Life is about experiences, not things.

Every personal detail Harry has ever put in writing. If a detail is not here, it does not go in

### The spine, in order

**The anchor dates.** Confirmed by Harry, 26 August 2026. Everything below hangs off these, so
check any new timeline claim against them first.

- **About seven years ago:** got married, then he and his wife both quit their jobs and went
  travelling. Before kids.
- **About six years ago:** first kid born.
- **About three years ago:** second kid born.
- **Six years ago until recently:** the gap. No inner work, no meditation. Two kids under school
  age. **This is the chaos period, and it is the reason the practice stopped.**
- **Recently:** out of the toddler years, and back into self-development and meditation.

1. **Leading into it, before kids: lost his sense of self.** Nothing dramatic, no crisis. Woke up
   one day with no real sense of who he was or what he was for. Was meditating a lot at the time
   and properly challenging his own sense of self and purpose. Went looking for something true,
   and feeling lost turned up first. **About a year with questions and no answer.** Symptoms:
   checked what everyone else wanted before answering what he wanted; things he used to love felt

2. **About seven years ago: got married, then both quit their jobs and went travelling for six
   months.** He and his wife did the same thing at the same time. No real plan. **A big leap of
   faith, taken together.** Still the biggest risk he has ever taken, and it was a gut decision,
   not a confident one.

   **This was not a solo decision, and the order matters:** married first, then both jobs went,
   then they left. Do not write it as a single man walking away from a life, and do not write the
   wife in as a passenger. She quit her job too.

3. **On the trip:** meditation and looking inward instead of outward changed how he saw
   everything. Came home **wanting to experience life, not just live it**.

4. **About six years ago: first kid. About three years ago: second kid.** Threw himself into being

5. **The six years from the first kid until recently: stuck.** Low mood, going through the

   dramatic cause. This is the "six stuck years" referenced across the site, and it runs **from

6. **Why it lasted six years, and why it ended when it did: the chaos period.** Two kids under
   school age is a genuine capacity limit, not a failure of will. The practice was the first thing
   to go, and stuck followed from the practice stopping. He has only started taking real action
   again **because he is out of the toddler years**, not because he finally found the discipline.

   This is the honest mechanism behind the whole origin story, and it matters for two reasons.
   It stops the six years reading as six wasted years he should have handled better. And it is
   the single most relatable thing on this list for any parent reading the site.

7. **Went back to the work.** Back to meditation and looking inward. No plan, just the knowledge
   that the version of him who had looked inward had been happier and more himself.

8. **The three books, in this order:**
   - *Letting Go: The Pathway of Surrender* (Hawkins), changed his whole perspective. Gave him a
     technique he could use daily.
   - *Truth vs Falsehood* (Hawkins), put life in context, put him properly back on the path.
   - *The 5am Club* (Robin Sharma), turned the first two into an actual daily practice.

9. **Work:** same line of work for **twelve years, on and off, with the travel gap in the
   middle**. Quietly lost interest. No breakdown, no signed-off-sick moment. A slow fade he
   mistook for how work was supposed to feel. This was burnout in its quiet form.

10. **Now:** works in **sales**, and loves it, because he gets to directly help people through
   honest conversations. It surprised him. He would not have predicted it.

### The why

- **His two kids are his why.** They are the reason he does everything.
- They keep him grounded, humble, and actually growing instead of standing still.
- Without them he does not think he would have a pivot point to aim energy at.
- He wants to give them **the most experiences he possibly can**: travel, live music, films, and

### The daily practice (exact, do not round or embellish)

- Gets up at **6 or 6:30am**. **Not 5am.** He says this explicitly and often.
- **20 minutes** meditation focused on letting go (not general clear-your-mind).
- **20 minutes** yoga.
- **20 minutes** reading books that help him grow. Not news, not email.
- **Fallback:** if the morning is gone, the same work before bed. Twenty minutes of letting-go
  meditation at the end of a hard day keeps the practice alive. This is a valid version, not a
  failure.
- Meditation method: sit in a chair or on a cushion, hands face up on the lap, eyes closed,
  breathe normally, **count every breath to ten**, then start again. When you lose count, smile
  and go back to one.

1. Be calm, relaxed and non-judgemental.
2. Money is everywhere and I do not need to chase it.
3. Be curious, ask questions.
4. Help people and give what I can.
5. Be accountable in my own life.
6. Be truthful in all areas of my life and to myself.
7. Be kind.
8. You do not own negative thoughts. It is a choice to believe them.

He always tells people **not to copy his list**. A borrowed list reads like advice, and advice is

### His wife is in the travelling story. Decision closed, site updated.

Harry confirmed on 26 August 2026 that she goes in. Every telling across the site was updated the
same day. **The travelling story is now a joint story and must always be written as one.**

**The canonical version:**

> Before I had kids, my wife and I got married. Then we both quit our jobs and went travelling for
> six months. We had no real plan. It was a big leap of faith, and we took it together.

**The rules that come with it**

- **Married first, then both jobs, then they left.** Always that order.
- **She quit her job too.** Never write her as a passenger, a supporter, or someone who came
  along. She made the same call at the same time.
- **Never write him as a single man walking away from a life.** That version is now wrong.
- **Never invent detail about her.** No name, no job, no dialogue, no description of what she
  thought, felt, feared or hoped. Only the four confirmed facts: they married first, she quit her
  job too, they went together, and it was a leap of faith for both of them.
- Referring to his own half of it is still fine. "Quitting my job felt frightening and open at the
  same time" is accurate, because he did quit his job.

**Why the joint version is better, and worth leaning into.** A solo gut call only costs you if it
goes wrong. This one was not only his to get wrong, and they took it anyway. That raises the
stakes of the story rather than softening them, and it is the strongest available evidence for
belief 12, that self-belief is the belief you will do what you say you will.

### Phrasing to prefer from now on

- **Not** "Then I had two kids. About six years later, I was stuck." That reads as six years after
  both kids, which is now wrong.
- **Instead** "Then my first was born, and for the six years since, the practice was gone."
- Existing posts use the older phrasing in five places. It is loose rather than false, so leave it
  unless a post is being edited anyway.

## 4. Voice

### Who is speaking

One person, writing in the first person, who is further along the same road as the reader and
says so. Not an expert. Not a guru. Someone who got stuck, found a way out, and is handing it over.

### The register

- **Plain English.** Simple words anyone can understand. No spiritual jargon. If a term like
  "surrender" or "the levels" is unavoidable, explain it in plain words immediately.
- **Short sentences.** Paragraphs of one to three lines. White space is part of the voice.
- **Reading level:** aim around a 5th grade / age 11 reading age. Test it.
- **A kind, wise friend talking.** Warm and human, never clinical.
- **Honest hedging.** "I think", "my honest read", "what I think is actually going on". Harry does
  not claim certainty he has not earned. He is explicit that this is not a diagnosis.
- **Anti-hype.** He names what does not work, admits what he still gets wrong, and says when
  something is hard. "It took me weeks of practice before it started to feel natural, and I still
  do not always manage it."
- **He refuses to overpromise.** "A checklist does not make you win every day. Anyone promising
  otherwise is selling something."

### Mechanics

- **UK English.** realise, recognise, practise (verb) / practice (noun), judgement, apologise,
  behaviour, colour.
- **No em dashes and no en dashes, ever.** Use commas, full stops, or separate sentences. This is
  a hard rule and it is the fastest tell that copy was not written by Harry.
- **One question at a time** in any conversational or coaching copy. Never stack two or three.
- **End with one focus**, not a list of things to do.

### The standard blog structure

Every long-form post follows this shape:

1. Hook, two to four short paragraphs, first person, a real moment
2. **TL;DR** box, one dense paragraph, the whole argument in plain terms
3. **In this article** contents list
4. H2 sections, each one idea, each ending with something to actually do
5. One or two inline CTA blocks ("Want more of this kind of thing?" and "Want to talk this through
   with someone?")
6. Internal links to at least three other posts, worked into the sentence, never a bare list
7. **Common questions about X**, a genuine FAQ with honest answers, including the awkward ones
8. Sign-off: "Written by Harry. Not a trained life coach or counsellor, just sharing what has
   helped me on my own journey."

### Signature phrases (his, use them)

- "You are enough as you are."
- "Stuck is not the same as lost."
- "Doing comes first. Belief follows after."
- "It was never yours to hold onto in the first place."
- "Suppression wearing a nicer outfit."
- "Not a feeling you wait for. Evidence you build."
- "It is like going to the gym."
- "No sales pitch, ever."
- "A real reply from me, not a funnel."
- "Feel it, release it, welcome something better in."
- "Drift is reversible."

## 5. Audience

**Who this is for:** UK adults, broadly 25 to 50, who overthink every decision, second-guess
themselves, and feel **stuck rather than lost**. Not in crisis. Nothing dramatic to point at.
Google searchers, not therapy seekers.

**The three problems, as Harry frames them** (this is the homepage framing, keep it consistent):

1. **You overthink every decision.** His read: this is the mind defending a position, needing to
   be certain and needing to be right, rather than actually weighing the choice. The need for
   certainty is a feeling you have not let go of, not a fact about the decision.
2. **Self-doubt drowns out what you know.** His read: self-doubt is a false reality, a leftover
   perspective built from old fears rather than evidence about who you are.
3. **You feel stuck, not lost.** His read: stuck happens when you stop doing the daily work of
   letting go, so the negative quietly piles up until it is running the show.

**What they are NOT:** they are not broken, not lazy, not lacking willpower, and not missing a
piece of themselves that has to be found. Never write to them as if they are.

**Tone toward them:** as equals, from slightly further down the same road. Never down to them.

## 6. Congruence traps

**The most important section in this document.** These are the places where standard self-help
advice, and the default output of most writing tools, directly contradicts Harry. Check every
draft against this list.

| The default advice | Why it is wrong here | Write this instead |
|---|---|---|
| "Just take action, confidence follows" | Wrong order. It works until the thoughts stop you leaving the house. | Guard the thought, replace it with a truth, then act to prove it. |
| "Be more disciplined" | Discipline is force and starvation. It runs on a negative belief about yourself and it always cracks. | Self-respect and accountability held out of love, not punishment. |
| "Push through", "no excuses", "be harder on yourself" | This is the language of managing an enemy, and the enemy is you. | Lower the bar, do the smaller version, keep the promise. |
| "Find your purpose / find your passion / find yourself" | Points outward, and assumes something is missing. | You do not need to find anything. You are already enough. |
| "Use affirmations" | An affirmation is a claim. If part of you does not believe it, it starts an argument in your own head. | Instructions, not claims. "Be curious" beats "I am confident". |
| "Fake it till you make it" | Performing is exactly the people-pleasing Harry had to let go of. | Drop the performance. People find you more interesting, not less. |
| "Wake up at 5am" | He gets up at 6 or 6:30 and says so. | Protect an hour before the day claims it. The clock time does not matter. |
| "Reframe your negative thoughts" | Reframing something you have not released is suppression wearing a nicer outfit. | Let go first, then reframe into the space that opens up. |
| "Make a big change / overhaul your life" | Overhauls rely on motivation, and motivation dips. | Twenty minutes a day, meaningful to you, done daily. |
| "Meditation is always good for you" | It can make you feel worse, and there are real stop signals. | Say so honestly, and give the stop signals (see Section 7). |
| "Trust your gut" as a standalone tip | Gut needs evidence behind it before it is trustworthy. | Fear closes you down, a genuine gut pull opens something up even when it is frightening. |
| "Self-doubt means you are not ready" | Doubt spikes near change, not near danger. | The volume tells you proximity, not accuracy. |
| "Set SMART, realistic goals" | He says aim high, reach for the stars, and build the goal around who you want to become. | Time-bound and specific, yes. Small and safe, no. |
| Stacking testimonials and social proof | Nothing is being sold, so there is no funnel to build proof for. | One review, already on the homepage. That is enough. |

## 7. Boundaries and disclaimers (hard rules, legal and ethical)

These appear in some form on every substantial page. Never drop them to make copy flow better.

**Always state:**
- Harry is **not a trained life coach or counsellor**. He can only help through the lens of what
  has actually helped him.
- If someone needs trained professional support, they should seek it out, **alongside this rather
  than instead of it**.
- Qualifications are BSc Sports Therapy and MEd, and **neither is a coaching or counselling
  qualification**.

**Never do:**
- Never diagnose. Never use clinical language as if it were assessment.
- Never claim to cure, heal, or treat anything.
- Never promise an outcome or a timeline for someone else's life.
- Never measure anyone's consciousness, give them a number, or reference muscle testing.
- Never present the Hawkins material as science.

**Meditation safety (must appear whenever meditation is taught at length):**
Sitting still removes the distractions someone was using to avoid what is underneath, so it can
surface. Boredom, restlessness, sadness, old memories and tearfulness are common and usually
settle over a few weeks. Brown University research found side effects are far more common than
apps suggest, and a minority of people get effects serious enough to affect daily functioning.
**Stop and get support** for: panic that will not settle, feeling detached from yourself or
reality, reliving trauma, inability to sleep, or inability to function. For anything milder, go
shorter and gentler rather than quitting.

**Burnout:** if burnout is severe, tell people to speak to a GP. This is not medical advice.

## 8. The business model (and what it forbids)

**The model:** free content, a free weekly email, and a direct reply from Harry. That is all of it.

**Why free, in his words:** the more he does this work, the more he wants to give instead of take.
Helping other people with what helped him is part of his own journey, not a lead magnet for
something he is trying to sell later.

**Therefore, never write:**
- Pricing, packages, tiers, or "investment"
- Booking flows, discovery calls, or "book a call"
- Paid-programme language, launches, waitlists, or scarcity
- A funnel of any kind, or a drip sequence that builds to a pitch
- Anything that implies the free content is a preview of paid content

**Always keep:**
- "No sales pitch, ever."
