The Catalyst Method
30 Day Challenge

Thirty days of proving to yourself that when you say you will do something, you do it.

About 25 minutes a day No app, no kit
Start here

You are not broken. Nothing is missing from you.

This is not 30 days of fixing yourself. It is 30 days of proving something to yourself.

Here is what you will prove. When you say you will do something, you do it.

That is what self belief really is. It is not a good mood. It is proof that your word is good.

I am not going to promise you will feel confident on day 31. Nothing works that fast. Anyone who says it does is selling you something.

What I can tell you is what happened to me. And that I still do this today.

What it costs you

About 25 minutes a day. Twenty for your activity. Under a minute to read your list. A few seconds to tick a box.

What you need

The tick sheet, and somewhere to write. That is it. Nothing to buy, no app to download.

How it works

Four phases, one habit.

The month is built so the hard parts arrive only once you have something to handle them with.

Days 0 to 4

Set up and start

Pick your twenty minutes and start on day one. The thinking gets layered on top of the doing, not before it.

Days 5 to 15

The run

Nothing new for eleven days. Just the habit, twice-daily list, and a tick. This is where it gets real.

Days 16 to 25

Letting go

Whatever the quiet brought up, you learn how to put it down instead of carrying it.

Days 26 to 30

Backing yourself

You count the proof, look honestly at where you are, and decide how you treat yourself from here.

Day0

Get ready

Three small jobs. None of them take long.

1. Pick your twenty minutes

Pick one thing you will do every day for 30 days.

  • Walk, with no phone and no music
  • Meditate
  • Run
  • Lift weights
  • Read something that helps you grow (reading list below)
  • Learn a new skill

Pick something you actually like. Do not pick it because you think you should. If you force it, you will stop by day three.

Not sure? Walk with no phone. It is the easiest way in. There is nothing to hide behind.

3. Write down your big goal

Not a thing you want. The person you want to be.

Aim high. Higher than feels sensible. Aim high and miss, and you still land somewhere better than if you aimed low.

Do not know what it is?

That is fine. Most people do not. It is often the reason they are here. Try one of these instead.

  • Write what you do not want your life to look like in five years.
  • Or write the three things that matter most to you. Put them in order. Use number one.
  • Or finish this sentence. "I want to be someone who..."

This is a first draft. It is not a promise. A rough goal you write today beats a perfect one you never write.

Day1

Start

Do your twenty minutes first. Then tick the box.

I mean that. Do the thing before you read the rest of this.

You already know how to think about your life. Thinking is not your problem. Doing is where this starts.

Then set your goal for the 30 days

Pick one goal for the month. Make it small. Make it something only you control, so nobody else's choices can take it from you.

Then do this

Picture it done

Picture your goal, and yourself having done it. Do not strain. Just see it done, and see yourself calm about it.

Loosen your grip

Notice how tightly you are holding on. Let it go a little. Wanting something too badly wears you out.

Decide you will do it

That is your intention. The wanting loosens. The intention stays.

Two honest notes

Step 2 is the easy version on purpose. Letting go properly is a skill and you learn it on day 17.

And if picturing your goal makes you feel silly, that is normal. A voice might say "that will never happen." Do not fight it, and do not force the picture. Write the thought down and keep it. You will use it on day 16.

Day2

Your daily list

Twenty minutes. Tick the box.

Now you are going to write a short list of how you want to behave. Keep it in your phone notes, pinned to the top.

First, one important thing

You are about to think about your bad days. So know this before you start.

Being hard on yourself says: "I am lazy and I always have been."
Being honest says: "I snapped at my sister on Tuesday."

One is a judgement about who you are. The other is a fact you can do something about. If a line you write says something bad about who you are, bin it.

You can use mine as a guide

Have a look at the shape of it. See how short the lines are. It walks you through writing yours, step by step.

Then put it away and write your own. Your own words carry weight. Someone else's read like advice, and advice is easy to ignore.

Open the checklist guide

Five to eight lines. Write things you can do, not things you have to believe.

This works

"Be curious." Something you can do in your next chat, whatever mood you are in.

This does not

"I am confident." A claim. If part of you does not believe it, you have started an argument with yourself.

Write it from your bad days. Think about the last few times you did not like how you acted. What would you have needed to remember right then? That is your first line.

Then cut it down. You should be able to say it from memory. A list you do not read is useless.

Day3

Three rules

Twenty minutes. List morning and night. Tick.

You read these today because they are for bad days. You cannot decide what to do on a bad day while you are having one.

Rule 1

Shrink it. Do not skip it.

Some days twenty minutes feels like too much. On those days do five. Five minutes still counts. Twenty is the target, not the minimum.

Rule 2

Miss a day? Carry on.

Go to the next day. Do not start again at day 1. Missing one day does not cancel the days you did. Starting again is a punishment, and punishment is what makes people quit.

Rule 3

If something hard comes up

Name it. Find it in your body, chest, stomach, throat or jaw. Let it sit there without fixing it until it fades. That is the short version of day 17.

Day4

Being honest with yourself

Twenty minutes. Tick the box.

Now five minutes of reading. Nothing to do today.

1. Being honest is not being hard on yourself

Honesty is the part people skip. Usually because they mix it up with having a go at themselves.

All month, check which one you are doing. If it ends in a word about who you are, that is you having a go at yourself. If it ends in a fact, that is honesty.

2. You cannot fix what you will not look at

That is why honesty comes first.

3. Being humble just means being willing to grow

It does not mean thinking less of yourself. It means accepting two things. You have growing to do. And you might be wrong about some of what you believe about yourself.

Part of you will fight this. Your ego wants you to stay the same. Same job, same friends, same you. Staying the same feels safe. So when that voice turns up, it will sound sensible. It usually does.

What today is not

Today is not the day you list everything wrong with your life. That would just hand your worst thoughts a shopping list. The real look comes on day 26, once you have put some things down.

Days5 to 15

Just keep going

Every morning and every night, read your list. Every day, do your twenty minutes. Every day, tick the box.

That is all. Nothing new for eleven days, and that is on purpose. The value is not in one good session. It is in doing it again and again. It works like the gym.

Some days it will feel useful. Some days it will feel like twenty minutes of fidgeting. Both count.

Read this now, because it is coming

Around day six or seven, this will start to feel pointless. The new feeling will have worn off. Nothing will look different.

Then a thought will turn up, and it will sound very reasonable. It will say: this is not working. Same as always.

That is scheduled. It happens to everyone. It is not a fact about you, and it is not a fact about whether this works.

What gets you through, 1

Look at your ticks, not your feelings

How you feel on day seven proves nothing. Six ticks proves something. That is why you keep the sheet.

What gets you through, 2

Use rule 1, not the door

On the days it feels worst, do five minutes. Five minutes on a bad day is worth more than twenty on an easy one. It proves you have a floor.

Get through days six to nine and you get through this. That stretch is the whole test, and it is far more boring than it feels.
Day16

Look back

Twenty minutes. Tick. Then this.

Sit down and look back at the last two weeks. How has your head actually felt? What keeps coming up that you need to deal with?

Write down the ones that keep coming back. Not everything, and not the worst thing you can think of. Just the ones that turned up more than once while you were walking, or the ones you noticed yourself steering away from.

Five or six is plenty. You have nine days and ten minutes a day to work through this. A list of forty is not better, it is unusable. Anything you leave off will come back on its own if it matters.

Write the honest version. Not the nice one, not the harsh one. Facts about what happened, not judgements about who you are. Nobody else is reading this, so a tidied up version only fools you.

Keep this page. You will compare it on day 26.

Is your list bigger than you expected?

That happens. Two weeks of quiet brings up more than people think it will.

If what you have written down feels bigger than a walk and ten minutes a day, say so. Send me the short version of it.

Day17

Letting go

Read this first

Sitting still takes away the things you use to distract yourself, so what is underneath comes up. Feeling bored, restless, sad or teary is common. Old memories can turn up. This usually settles over a few weeks.

Researchers at Brown University found side effects are far more common than the apps admit. A small number of people get effects bad enough to affect daily life.

Stop, and get support, if you:

  • get panic that will not settle
  • feel detached from yourself or from the world
  • start reliving something traumatic
  • cannot sleep, or cannot function

For anything milder, go shorter and gentler. Do not force it.

What to do

Sit for ten minutes. Bring one thing from your day 16 list to mind. Let the feeling actually come up rather than just thinking about it.

Then breathe, and let it go. Let go of the feeling, and let go of what you decided it means.

It was never yours to carry.

Do not fight it. Do not talk yourself out of it. Do not explain it. Let it sit there until it runs out of steam. It always does.

You will probably think you are doing it wrong

Almost everyone does. You will sit down, follow the steps, and feel nothing move. Then you will decide you are bad at this.

It took me weeks before it felt natural. I still do not always manage it.

You cannot do it badly enough to matter, because the worst case is you sat quietly for ten minutes. And feeling nothing does not mean it failed. Some of it goes with no moment you can point to.

Stuck on this bit?

This is the part people find hardest, and it is the hardest part to learn from a page.

If you have sat with it a few times and nothing is moving, do not spend another week guessing. Tell me what you are doing and what is happening when you do it.

Days18 to 25

Do it daily

Ten minutes letting go, plus your twenty. Tick.

Work through your day 16 list, one thing at a time. Two extra things happen in this stretch.

Day 20. What happened, and what you added

After your ten minutes, pick one thing from your list. Split a page in two.

What actually happened

Just the facts. What a camera would have seen.

What you added

What you decided it meant. What you decided it said about you.

Look at which side is longer. Most of what hurts is on the right. And the right side is not the event, it is how it looks from where you are standing today. Where you are standing is not the same as what is real.

Day 22. Guard the thought, then swap it

By now you are noticing things. But noticing is not enough on its own. You need to know what to do in the moment. The whole thing takes about a minute.

Guard the door

A thought turns up. "They will think I am an idiot." Do not let it walk straight in. Stop, and notice you are about to believe it. Most doubting thoughts get in because nobody checked them.

Swap it for something true

Not a nicer lie. Do not swap "I am rubbish at this" for "I am brilliant at this," because part of you knows that is not true. Swap it for something you can stand behind: "That is a feeling, not a fact." "I have done harder things." "I do not know how this will go yet."

Do one small thing that proves it

A true thought with nothing behind it fades by the afternoon. Send the message. Ask the question. Put your hand up. Small is fine. The point is the proof, not the size.

Why this comes now, and not in week one. Swapping a thought before you have let the feeling go is just covering it with a nicer sentence. The feeling is still there and it comes back. You have spent five days letting go, so there is room now.

Day26

Look back and count

Write down how you feel now, and what has been coming up lately. Then read your day 16 page and put them side by side. You are not marking yourself. You are looking to see if the right hand side has got shorter.

Now count your ticks

Read this before you count

Whatever the number is, it is a count of times you kept your word to yourself. That is all it is. It is not a score, and there is no number on that sheet that means you failed.

Got 25? Good. Got 14? Then you kept your word to yourself fourteen times in three weeks. That is fourteen more than the you who had not started.

Now watch what your mind does. It will take a real number and turn it into a verdict. "Not even half. Same as always. Knew it." That is you having a go at yourself.

Honesty says: I did it fourteen times out of twenty three. Everything after that is a story you added.

The mirror

Holding yourself up to a mirror means looking at where you actually are. Not where you say you are. Not where you meant to be by now.

That sounds harsh. It is the opposite. When you respect yourself, you can afford to be honest. The truth stops being a threat. You could not have done this on day 4, which is why I did not ask.

Two questions. Answer them plainly.

  1. Where am I really, on the thing I set out to do on day 1?
  2. What have I been avoiding looking at?

And this is not the soft option. Forcing yourself through something is easy to understand. Sitting with why you avoided it is harder, and doing that without turning it into an attack on yourself is harder still.

Days27 to 30

Treating yourself well

The sitting changes now. You are not working through your list any more. Instead, sit with this question.

If I really loved myself, would I treat my body, my mind and my time like this?

Then picture it. What would treating yourself well actually look like, in real life, in small ordinary detail? And what would you need to change to make that true? Write down what you come up with.

Yours will be your own. But here are the kinds of things that come up.

Where you spend your days

Who you spend time with. Some people help you grow. Some quietly keep you where you are.

What you eat

Moving your body

Doing more of what you love

Helping other people

None of that is a rule and nothing here is banned. That is the point. If you were being strict with yourself, you would be banning things. Here it works the other way. You stop doing what harms you, because that is not how you treat someone you care about.

One extra job on day 27

Look ahead to day 29 and decide now what your one real thing is going to be. Write it down.

Decide it today. If you leave it until the day, you will pick something safe, or find a reason it is not the right time. Make it small and clear. Not "be more confident at work." One message. One question. One chat. One thing you send.

Day 29. Do the real thing

Do the short version first. Notice the feeling, find it in your body, let it sit there until it fades, then check what actually happened against what you added on top. Then do the thing anyway.

This has a shrink rule too. If the thing you picked really will not move today, do a smaller version. Send the shorter message. Ask the smaller question. Say one sentence instead of having the whole chat.

A small version done beats the full version put off. And putting it off is a move you already know well.

If you do not manage it at all, that is not the month undone. You have 28 days of proof behind you, and one day you did not take. Write down honestly what stopped you.

Day30

The promise

Twenty minutes. Tick the last box.

Read it all back. Your day 16 page, your day 26 page, and your list from the last four days. Count your ticks one more time. All 30 days.

Now make one promise to yourself. Promise to treat yourself with love and respect.

Then plan it. Go through your list from days 27 to 30 and decide what you are actually going to do. Not all of it. Pick what matters and start there.

That promise is the whole thing. It is the engine that keeps everything else running.

If you force yourself instead, it will crack. Usually around week six. Then you will decide the crack says something about you. It does not. You just ran out of something that always runs out.

Holding yourself to something out of love holds. Holding yourself to it out of shame does not. Same actions. Completely different engine. Only one of them still works in a year.

The reading list

The books that actually did something for me.

This is not homework. You do not need to read all of them. Any one on its own is enough. If you only ever read one, read the first.

1

Letting Go: The Pathway of Surrender

David R. Hawkins

This is the one. Start here. It gave me a way to put a feeling down instead of carrying it round all day. Everything in this challenge from day 17 onwards came out of this book.

2

Truth vs Falsehood

David R. Hawkins

This one changed how I see other people. It taught me that how I see things is not the same as how things are. That sounds simple. It took me a long time to really feel it.

3

The 5am Club

Robin Sharma

The practical one. Give yourself an hour a day before the world gets to you. That is where your twenty minutes came from. You do not have to get up at 5am. I get up at 6 or 6:30.

4

Transcending the Levels of Consciousness

David R. Hawkins

A map of the states people move through. Shame and guilt at the bottom, fear, anger and pride in the middle, then courage, and above that acceptance, love and peace. It gave me words for something I had been going round in circles about for years.

5

Mindfulness: A Practical Guide to Awakening

Joseph Goldstein

Long, and worth it. Go here when you want to understand what you are actually doing when you sit still.

6

The Dhammapada

Translated by Eknath Easwaran

Short verses. You can read one page and put it down. I keep coming back to this one. Easwaran's introduction is worth the book on its own.

One honest note on number 4

Hawkins' wider work includes muscle testing, and putting numbers on people and books and countries. I do not use that part and I do not teach it. I use the levels as a shared language, and as a direction to walk in. Nothing more.
